## Most likely causes

### 1. Torque converter clutch shudder (81% of likelihood among these)

*System:* Transmission  |  *DIY difficulty:* Advanced - special tools or lifting the vehicle

**Why:** Shudder that only shows up warm at a steady, light-throttle cruise is the classic converter-clutch pattern.

**Checks you can do:**

1. Check transmission fluid level and condition if your vehicle has a dipstick (red/pink and not burnt-smelling is good).
2. Note the exact speed it happens. If a light tap of the brake (without slowing) or a slight throttle change makes it stop instantly, that points to the converter clutch.
3. Scan for codes, including pending codes.

**When to call a pro:** Fluid service or software updates often need a shop scan tool. Internal converter problems are a shop job.

### 2. Engine misfire (plugs, coils, or injector) (10% of likelihood among these)

*System:* Engine  |  *DIY difficulty:* Moderate - some mechanical experience

**Why:** Shaking with stumbling or a check engine light most often comes from ignition parts.

**Checks you can do:**

1. Scan for codes. P0301-P0308 tell you which cylinder.
2. If one cylinder is misfiring and your engine has coil-on-plug, swap that coil with a neighbor. If the misfire follows the coil, replace the coil.
3. Pull and inspect the spark plug in that cylinder. Look for oil, cracks, or a worn electrode.

**When to call a pro:** If the check engine light is flashing, stop driving: unburned fuel can overheat and ruin the catalytic converter.

### 3. Driveline, tire, or wheel balance vibration (10% of likelihood among these)

*System:* Tires/Driveline  |  *DIY difficulty:* Moderate - some mechanical experience

**Why:** Vibration that follows road speed rather than engine load usually comes from the tires, wheels, or driveshaft.

**Checks you can do:**

1. Look for uneven tire wear, bulges, or missing wheel weights.
2. Check whether the vibration depends on speed (same at any throttle) or on load. Speed-only usually means tires, balance, or the driveshaft.
3. With the vehicle safely on jack stands, check u-joints for play by twisting the driveshaft by hand.

**When to call a pro:** Tire balancing needs a shop machine. Any play in the u-joints or a wobbling tire should be fixed before highway driving.
